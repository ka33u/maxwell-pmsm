# Verification and reporting gates

Read when reviewing results or planning angle, mesh, time-step, period or loss studies. Distinguish quick screening from a user-requested accuracy or acceptance study; record actual tests and incomplete items rather than implying checks occurred.

## Run evidence

- Check a case's actual `run_result.txt` for `Validation=1`, solver normal completion, saved project and close; inspect warnings and verify a nonempty, parseable CSV with finite values, expected units and required time coverage. Exit status or a saved project alone is insufficient. Compare inputs and configuration/script hashes; record engine version and source project identity. Never silently turn a failed Maxwell solve into an analytical estimate. For no-load/load/loss cases generated by the local workflow, `python <skill>/scripts/audit_case.py --case <case-dir> --kind noload|load|loss` performs a read-only minimum evidence check; inspect its JSON and limitations. It is intentionally limited to those known outputs and does not establish convergence, material correctness or physical validity.
- In the supported case layout, an empty solution-results directory, an incomplete source path/hash pair, or a nonintegral configured cycle count is rejected. A nonempty results directory is only a structural check: it does not prove that usable solver data belongs to this case. An unavailable source path remains a warning and cannot be treated as verified lineage; inspect the audit output rather than relying on its status alone.
- The configuration and runner may embed SHA-256 checks, but a log saying a check passed does not independently prove the current file still matches what ran. Compare recorded manifest hashes where available and retain the raw files.
- Compare phase EMF fundamentals, total RMS, phase sequence and period; record a complete window and whether the first/last points match. Verify current values and sign, plus torque mechanical power and induced electrical power with clear sign conventions. Their difference is not automatically loss.
- For the supported positive synchronous, ABC local case layout, the optional [offline dq consistency check](dq-check.md) compares transformed flux/current torque with the solver waveform. It does not itself identify Ld/Lq or validate the rotor magnetic axis. A common rotation of the d/q axes changes individual components while leaving the power inner product and flux-current cross product unchanged; agreement of those quantities cannot establish the axis offset.

## Convergence and operating point

- Use identical operating point and physical output window when comparing mesh or time-step variants. Refine the air gap/bridges and, separately, the magnet interior for magnet eddy loss. A single Band edge length or a `Validation=1` is not convergence. Compare torque average and ripple, EMF, local peak B and each requested loss; choose and document task-appropriate tolerances, both values and relative/absolute differences. Avoid arbitrary acceptance thresholds without user/project criteria.
- For supported local generated load cases, `scripts/audited_compare_loaded.py` runs `audit_case.py` for each case and requires the same accessible, hash-verified source project. It accepts only runners that are byte-identical after normalizing their single independently checked configuration digest, then delegates numeric comparison to the explicitly provided, trusted workspace comparator. It records audits, solver warnings and tool hashes. Inspect those warnings; this does not establish runner authenticity, physical validity, convergence or equality of unrecorded model physics. Missing or inaccessible source or differently generated runners cause refusal rather than an assumed equivalent comparison. See [local toolchain](local-toolchain.md).
- Ansys's [2025 R1 solution-data documentation](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/views/secured/electronics/v251/en/subsystems/maxwell/content/Maxwell/ViewingSolutionData.htm) states that transient solutions do not provide adaptive mesh-convergence data; do not interpret a missing convergence table as a passing check. Verify the behavior for the installed version and make independent controlled mesh comparisons. The [2026 R1 2D transient setup documentation](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/views/secured/electronics/v261/en/subsystems/maxwell/content/Maxwell/TransientSetup2D.htm) describes adaptive time steps and external-circuit time-step control; record the actual solved time grid and controller rather than assuming the nominal setup step is what was used. These newer documents are guidance, not proof of the 2021 R1 configuration or compatibility.
- Resolve angle against a known rotor/EMF phase and phase order. Check the sign of motor torque, scan the allowed range at rated current if optimization or rated-capability judgment is requested, and refine around a bracketed optimum. Include voltage/inverter and thermal constraints if claiming a feasible operating point. If only one angle is calculated, label it a single candidate.
- Number of electrical cycles per mechanical turn equals pole pairs. For example, 12 poles means six electrical cycles per mechanical turn. Compare two adjacent complete stable mechanical turns if the quantity and geometry require it; a shorter periodic sector is valid only when its symmetry and multipliers are proven. Merely comparing adjacent electrical cycles may miss slot, geometry or branch asymmetry.
- `python <skill>/scripts/compare_mechanical_periods.py --csv <waveform.csv> --column <exact-header> --poles <even-poles> --frequency-hz <f> --end-time-s <end> --out <new.json>` compares the last two full mechanical turns in a uniformly sampled, synchronous-speed waveform. It rejects missing/irregular samples and insufficient duration, reports time-weighted mean and paired waveform differences, and preserves source hashes. This is a measurement of two windows, not proof of convergence; inspect `--help` for time-unit and endpoint requirements. It must not be applied to non-synchronous operation by treating electrical frequency as shaft speed.

## Materials, loss and scope

- Use traceable B-H and multi-frequency loss data covering modeled B and frequency, with density, stacking, temperature and manufacturing conditions. When using substitute material, identify the actual and reference grades and fit range; report extrapolation and Maxwell B-H warnings. The model's loss formulation, field feedback, Kdc and steel eddy settings affect interpretation.
- Report stator and rotor core loss separately; test stability across appropriate windows and ensure their sum matches total. For PM eddy, identify enabled objects, net-current conditions, conductivity, interior mesh, step size, 2D end-current/axial segmentation limitations and `SolidLoss` scope. A short, changing loss history is preliminary even if the solver completed normally.
- Calculate DC copper loss separately from phase resistance at a known temperature, current and connection. AC copper, end winding, mechanical, stray and inverter losses require separate evidence. Do not compute whole-machine efficiency or temperature from a partial loss list.
- Frozen-permeability Ld/Lq, irreversible demagnetization, overload, NVH, thermal or structural studies need their own configuration and verification; these are not supplied automatically by the local generic scripts. Document requested-but-unavailable results rather than inventing them.
